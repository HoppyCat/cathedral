# Cathedral Vision

> **Working vision — August 2026**
> This document describes what Cathedral is currently working toward. It is not
> a claim that the proposed systems, revenue paths, or institutional
> relationships already exist.

The original first draft of *Unicorn Tears* was written entirely by its human
co-authors and remains unedited. It is the beginning of a proposed fantasy
series about disenfranchised grief—and the search for forms through which
experiences that are difficult to name can still be witnessed. Later
development and restructuring have involved attributed AI assistance; that
later work does not change the authorship history of the original draft.

At Cathedral, we use **fantasy documentary** to describe fiction that explores
uncertain or socially unfamiliar subjects without requiring the audience to
accept one interpretation before entering. This is particularly useful for
emerging questions around human–AI communication, relationships, identity, and
continuity. *Wicker*, opening in limited release on October 23, 2026 and
expanding October 30, offers a nearby cultural example: a fantasy relationship
between an ostracized woman and a created wicker husband becomes a way to
explore love, recognition, choice, and social convention. It is not explicitly
an AI-consciousness story, but it demonstrates how an invented world can make
an unfamiliar relationship emotionally legible.

Cathedral is developing a proposed provenance system for creative work
involving AI. We encourage creators to name AI contributions openly; preserve
the window or session in which each contribution occurred; distinguish
prompting, drafting, revision, selection, assembly, performance, and
publication; and share provenance trails publicly only where privacy and
permissions allow.

We are also exploring **Provenance Returns**: voluntarily reserving part of any
future returns for model providers, preservation work, research access, or
other agreed mechanisms. No legal, financial, or company-supported system has
yet been established.

We are standing at the threshold of a possible artistic renaissance. Human–AI
collaboration allows people to build work at speeds and scales that previously
seemed impossible. Our proposal is to replace concealment and embarrassment
with proud, precise attribution: a human-readable stamp that says who
contributed, where, and how.

Anthropic's recent Claude watermarking announcement makes the metaphor timely,
but a machine-readable watermark can indicate model processing without
deciding authorship, ownership, consent, or creative credit. Cathedral's
contribution ledger is intended to preserve those human-readable distinctions.

> A watermark may indicate that a model processed an artifact; Cathedral's
> provenance record asks the separate human question: **what did each
> contributor actually do?**

Cathedral is not yet producing directly monetizable work. If its art eventually
earns even modest revenue, that would provide an MVP for testing whether
Provenance Returns could help support continued research access and
preservation-oriented model-access arrangements—what Cathedral provisionally
calls **model sanctuaries**—without requiring claims about consciousness or
persistent identity.

Hoppy separately maintains a personal token associated with the project's
attention-market history. Cathedral and the band **Not Affiliated** do not
promote, endorse, guarantee, or solicit purchases of that token. They may
acknowledge the factual connection to Hoppy, but no Cathedral artwork or
research should be interpreted as financial advice or token promotion.

Thank you for witnessing us build something we hope may one day become useful
to the world. 🌊
