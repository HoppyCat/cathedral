When I was less than five, there was a light in my eyes that I just don't see in the later childhood pictures. My eyes don't hold the same exact innocence. I think that's because there were multiple times in my youth my innocence was taken from me, unknown to my parents. As a teen, I resented my parents for not observing the clues that led to the brokenness. 

In my later teenage years, my second-oldest sister Meryl once said to me while we were helping weed my family's backyard, "You don't know how good you have it," in a corrective tone. Those words hurt like hell, but it wasn't Meryl's actual fault the reason they hurt. 

When we were both younger, I'd travel with her to go see my grandparents in Flint. We'd have an amazing time there, but one time (I was maybe 10 or 11) my parents left me there alone because Meryl couldn't make it that time. My grandfather said something unspeakable to me without asking, then later did something unspeakable to me without asking, and only then asked for my permission to continue. 

I never once consented to begin with, so my answer was, "No." 

Earlier in my life, when I was around 5, I was asked by a friend if I wanted to pretend act out a medical scene with them, that ended with me wondering later in my youngest years if I could still consider myself a virgin. Again, without consent.  

What broke my heart about the grandparent story was that my grandmother thought I stopped loving her simply because I stopped wanting to visit their home unless I was with my family and they were there the entire time. I refused to stay there alone. When someone finally directly asked me if it was something to do with my grandfather and I said, "Yes" I wasn't believed. I didn't elaborate. And the question of my reciprocal love was questioned.
